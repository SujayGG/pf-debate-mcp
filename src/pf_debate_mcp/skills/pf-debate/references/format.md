# Public Forum format (NSDA)

Two teams of two debaters. A coin flip decides who picks either the side (Pro/Con) or the speaking order (first/second); the other team gets the remaining choice. Topics change during the season. The NSDA releases a new PF resolution for Sep/Oct, Nov/Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar and Nationals. Always confirm the current resolution before prepping (search "NSDA Public Forum topic <month year>").

## Speech order
| # | Speech | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Constructive (1st team) | 4:00 | 1st speaker, team A |
| 2 | Constructive (2nd team) | 4:00 | 1st speaker, team B |
| 3 | Crossfire | 3:00 | both 1st speakers |
| 4 | Rebuttal (1st team) | 4:00 | 2nd speaker, team A |
| 5 | Rebuttal (2nd team) | 4:00 | 2nd speaker, team B |
| 6 | Crossfire | 3:00 | both 2nd speakers |
| 7 | Summary (1st team) | 3:00 | 1st speaker, team A |
| 8 | Summary (2nd team) | 3:00 | 1st speaker, team B |
| 9 | Grand crossfire | 3:00 | all four |
| 10 | Final focus (1st team) | 2:00 | 2nd speaker, team A |
| 11 | Final focus (2nd team) | 2:00 | 2nd speaker, team B |
Prep time is 3:00 per team, usable before any of that team's speeches.

## Word budgets (spoken)
Lay pace is about 160 wpm and circuit pace 220+ wpm.

| Speech | Lay | Flow/circuit |
|---|---|---|
| Constructive (4:00) | 650–750 words | 850–1000 words |
| Rebuttal (4:00) | 650–750 | 900+ |
| Summary (3:00) | 480–550 | 650+ |
| Final focus (2:00) | 320–370 | 450 |

Card text counts only the **highlighted** words, plus the tag and short cite.

## What each speech must do
- **Constructive:** Read the case: contentions with cut cards, plus any framework or pre-emptive weighing.
- **1st rebuttal:** Attack the opponent's case only (no frontlining needed; nothing has hit you yet). Front-load turns and the best defense.
- **2nd rebuttal:** Attack their case AND **frontline** (answer their rebuttal against your case), at least on the argument(s) you will collapse to. Anything left unanswered here counts as conceded. The norm: second rebuttal must frontline turns and the arguments it will go for.
- **Summary:** Collapse to 1–2 pieces of offense. Extend each one fully (uniqueness, link, impact, with the card names). Frontline what the opponent said against it. Extend the defense/turns you need against their case. Start the weighing. Anything final focus uses must be in summary.
- **Final focus:** Mirror your summary: the same offense, the same weighing, the voting issues. No new arguments. In second final focus, answer only what first final focus said, using material already on the flow.
- **Crossfire:** Questions and answers. Nothing said in crossfire counts unless it is brought up in a speech. Use it to set up rebuttal or summary ("As they conceded in crossfire...").

## Speaking position strategy
- **First speaking:** Your summary has to cover the opponent's 2nd rebuttal frontlines. Your final focus answers nothing new, so pre-empt their weighing in summary.
- **Second speaking:** You see their full case before your rebuttal, and you get the last word. The cost is a heavy 2nd rebuttal (attack plus frontline in 4 minutes).

## Evidence norms (see evidence-ethics.md)
Any card read or referenced must be produced when asked, fast. Many circuits share speech docs by email chain or Speechdrop before speeches. The NSDA permits paraphrasing held to the full text's accuracy, but circuit and TOC norms strongly favor read cut cards, and paraphrase theory is common. Always build with real cut cards; the tools here only produce those.
