# Evidence ethics (NSDA rules + circuit norms)

Violations can lose the round (evidence challenge), the tournament, or worse. These rules bind the agent too.

## Hard rules
1. **Never write card text from memory or invent it.** Card text comes only from `get_card` (existing cards) or `fetch_source` followed by `cut_card`. If no source can be fetched, say so and tell the user what evidence is still needed. Never fill in with a "placeholder" card, a "sample" quote, or a remembered statistic.
2. **Verbatim.** The body is the author's exact words. `cut_card` enforces this; do not try to work around a REJECTED result by editing quotes. Re-read the source and copy it exactly.
3. **Full citation:** author, qualifications, date, title, publication, URL, and access date. If the auto-extracted author is missing, find it on the page or use the organization. Look up quals (the author bio or staff page) rather than guessing.
4. **No out-of-context cuts.** Do not start or stop a card where the author's meaning flips ("Critics claim X..." cut without the author's rebuttal). Do not highlight around negations ("not", "unlikely", "some argue") in a way that reverses the meaning.
5. **The tag must be supported by the highlighted text.** No powertagging: if the card says "could affect", the tag cannot say "causes".
6. **No clipping.** In a real round, the debater must read every highlighted word they claim to read.
7. **Paraphrase:** NSDA permits it only if it accurately represents the full text, and the original must be producible. These tools do not produce paraphrased evidence. Build with cut cards.
8. **Produce on request.** Every card must be retrievable. Keep the URL, and export to a speech doc.

## Quality bar for a good card
- Recent (the last 1–2 years for fast-moving topics like AI or energy), unless it's a theory or empirical classic.
- A qualified author (academic, think tank, government, major outlet reporter with a beat) over blogs and press releases.
- It contains a **warrant**, not just a claim.
- Underline about 30–50% of the body; highlight about 10–25%. The highlighted words read as grammatical sentences.
- The body is long enough for context: at least 2–3 sentences, often a full paragraph or more.

## Checking an opponent's evidence (indicts)
Compare the tag with the highlighted text (a powertag?). Read the unhighlighted text for concessions ("though most experts consider this unlikely"). Check the date, the quals, and whether the study methodology fits their claim. Ask for the full source in crossfire.
