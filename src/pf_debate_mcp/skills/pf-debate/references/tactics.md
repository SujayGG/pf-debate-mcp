# PF tactics that win rounds

## Case construction
- **Two contentions beat three.** Each needs a full chain (UQ, link, IL, impact) with a card on every step that isn't common sense. One well-warranted contention plus a strong framework/weighing contention is a common circuit shape.
- **Pick the terminal impact first**, then find the shortest defensible chain to it. Fewer links means higher probability, and probability weighing beats "many-link" nuke war scenarios in front of good judges.
- **Pre-empt weighing in the constructive** (a "framing" or "weighing" sub-point) so later speeches only extend it.
- **Build in turns-case.** A contention that also causes the opponent's likely impact wins the weighing debate for free.
- **Front-load the best card** on each step. Rebuttal usually attacks the internal link.
- **Neg on "should enact X" topics:** Contentions are disadvantages ("DA") to the action, sometimes plus solvency attacks. Aff: advantages plus a uniqueness case for why action is needed now.

## Rebuttal
- **Order:** turns first (they are offense), then the best defense, then weighing. Number responses ("three responses on their C1").
- **Answer the weakest link** in their chain with evidence, not just analysis.
- **A turn needs:** the claim, a card, and an impact ("...which means their C1 is now offense for us").
- **Don't double-turn yourself.** Never read a link turn and an impact turn on the same argument.
- **2nd rebuttal** splits roughly 2:00 on their case and 2:00 frontlining (at minimum, frontline the argument you will go for and every turn).

## Summary and final focus
- **Collapse.** Go for the single argument you are winning most cleanly, plus its weighing. Two arguments at most.
- **Extensions must include the warrant:** "Extend Smith 24: a moratorium cedes compute to China *because* hyperscale capacity is the binding constraint on frontier training. That means..." Card names alone are not an extension.
- **Frontline in summary** whatever they said against your collapse argument.
- **Kick out of turns properly.** Concede the defense that takes out your own argument, so their turn has no link.
- **Weigh comparatively:** "Even if they win their impact, we outweigh on probability because their scenario needs five steps and each has defense."
- **Final focus = summary's voting issues**, told as a story in the judge's words. No new material.
- **Dropped offense:** Extend it with the warrant, say explicitly that it was conceded, then weigh it. Conceded ≠ auto-win if it isn't weighed.

## Crossfire
- Ask short, closed questions that set up a speech. "Does your evidence say the moratorium is permanent?"
- Question banks: date and recency of their key card; author quals; "what's the internal link between X and Y?"; "is this impact unique?"; "what does your card's unhighlighted part say?"; "which is more probable, and why?"
- **Traps:** Get them to concede a premise that later links into your turn.
- **Answering:** Brief answers. Never concede a warrant. Pivot to your offense.
- **Grand crossfire:** Let the stronger speaker lead; don't talk over each other.

## Weighing that works
- **Probability vs magnitude:** Magnitude arguments (extinction) lose to good probability indicts (a long chain, no warrant for escalation, no empirical precedent). Pair any big impact with a probability defense ("nuclear deterrence has failed before, see Able Archer; risk compounds").
- **Timeframe** short-circuits: the impact that happens first can prevent the other.
- **Pre-req** and **turns case** are the strongest link-level weighing.
- **Meta-weigh** when both teams weigh on different mechanisms.

## Judge adaptation
- **Lay (parent) judge:** Fewer arguments, stories, plain English, no jargon, slower (about 160 wpm). Real-world examples beat card names. Use humanized impacts (lives, jobs, families). Explain nuke-war chains believably or avoid them. Use structural or probability framing.
- **Flow judge:** Signpost, number responses, extend warrants, and weigh explicitly. Moderate speed.
- **Tech / circuit judge:** Speed is OK and a speech doc is expected. Theory/Ks are possible. Dropped arguments are true. Big-stick impacts (nuke war, extinction) are accepted when the chain is carded. Evidence ethics get checked, and paraphrase theory is common.

## Prep strategy for a new topic
1. Define the resolution's key terms (find a definition card for each ambiguous word).
2. List the 3–5 strongest arguments on each side, each with its terminal impact.
3. Pull the most-read library cards for those impacts (`search_cards(..., sort="popular")`).
4. Write blocks to the top opposing arguments before perfecting your own case.
5. Scout the caselist after the first tournaments. Update blocks to what teams actually read.
